Experiment C: which extra Koios designs join (written 2026-11-17 01:12 EST, before any trial run
and before any pass-on run of an extra design)

Candidates: the 8 Koios designs in ~/sharc-lab/koios_blifs outside the 7 (synthesized with the 7 by
hardblocks/synth_koios.sh). Trials: reduction_layer, softmax, tpu_like.small.ws (10-30k LUTs, the
size of the 7), and lstm and dla_like.small (about 95k LUTs each). dnnweaver (114k LUTs, 1221 RAMs),
clstm_like.small (175k) and gemm_layer (396k) get no trial: each is bigger than lstm and
dla_like.small, so if those two fail the rule these fail it too; if lstm or dla_like.small passes,
they get a trial before the sweep starts.

Trial: seed 1, the split fabric with the pass off, SPICE delays (k = 1.0), routed clocks, exactly as
the sweep runs it: VPR's minimum-width search, then the route at 1.3x the minimum. VPR wrapped in
/usr/bin/time -l (hardblocks/vpr_timed.sh) for wall time and peak memory. Run with other VPR jobs
alongside (the B sweep), so times are pessimistic. Each VPR step is capped at 30 minutes
(--timeout 1800).

Rule: an extra design joins if its trial routes and takes at most 30 minutes of wall time in total
(search plus route) with at most 4 GB peak memory in any VPR step. Every design that passes is run
in full (10 seeds; stacked, split pass-off and split pass-on; routed and ideal clocks; k = 1.0 and
k = 1.62 + pins) and reported, with nothing dropped afterwards.

Why these limits: the slowest run of the 7 designs took 13.1 minutes and peaked at 1.72 GB
(HARDBLOCKS.md, "Cost of a full 10-seed sweep"). 30 minutes and 4 GB are about twice that. Each
design takes 40 width searches (10 seeds x split and stacked x 2 crossing points), so a design at
the limit costs about 20 VPR-hours, about 2 hours of wall time at 12 runs in parallel.

So dnnweaver, clstm_like.small and gemm_layer get no trial (see above), and the list is
reduction_layer, softmax and tpu_like.small.ws. tpu_like.small.ws is a close relative of
tpu_like.small.os (one of the 7): both are Koios's 16x16 TPU-like design; the .os source calls its
systolic unit output stationary, and .ws is, by its name, the weight-stationary version. It is not
an independent design in the way the other two are.
