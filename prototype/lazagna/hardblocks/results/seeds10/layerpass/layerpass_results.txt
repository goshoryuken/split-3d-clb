Timing-aware layer pass for the split CLB (branch layer-pass, 2026-11-16)

Question. The split CLB's critical paths cross layers about 3 times, stacked's under 1, which is why
slower crossings hurt the split. Ismael: VPR packs the split CLB's LUTs without regard to layer. Does a
post-pack pass that picks each FLE's layer by timing cut those crossings, and does the worst-case
edge over stacked (1.62x crossings plus the budgeted layout's pin-path cost) move?
Follow-ups (2026-11-17), at the end: A the failed runs rerun with more router iterations; B the
pass at 2.24x, 2.72x (LaZagna's default) and 3.0x; C three Koios designs the pass has never seen;
D the rewritten packing through OpenFPGA and simulation; E all 10 designs at every setting (C's 3
added at the other crossing delays, against 2D, in the ablation and at equal tracks) and a trial of
lstm and dla_like.small.

The pass (prototype/lazagna/layer_pass.py; TECHNICAL_MAP 1.2 and 2.4). After VPR packs, it moves
FLEs between each split CLB's two layers, keeping 10 FLE slots and 40 input pins per layer (each
layer's crossbar reads only its own pins; feedback reaches every FLE, so the crossbar is never the
limit). Cost per connection between two FLEs: (packer setup criticality)^8 x the crossing delay it
would pay, the in-CLB crossbar crossing inside a CLB or the routing crossing between CLBs, both read
from the arch. The criticality comes from VPR's own packer timing analysis, written by the new
--pack_criticality_file. RAM, DSP and IO connections count nothing (the placer picks their layer).
Search: best-gain moves and swaps per CLB, other CLBs fixed, repeated over all CLBs until a sweep
gains nothing (3-6 sweeps, about 2 s for conv_layer). CLBs where the packer ran a signal through
an empty FLE (LUT in wire mode, used when one layer's 40 pins are full: 5 of 615 CLBs in
eltwise_layer, none in conv_layer) are left as packed. Both crossing delays scale with k, so the
pass makes the same choice at every k; only what each removed crossing is worth changes.

Method (run_layerpass.sh). The split fabric only, 7 Koios designs x 10 seeds, routed and ideal
clocks, at k = 1.0 (SPICE, the headline), 1.62 and 1.62 + pins. Every run is its committed pass-off
twin with only --layer-pass added: same arch, seed, and the twin's exact track count (--widths), so
the layer assignment is the one thing that changes. Stacked cannot use the pass (one CLB per layer);
its rows are the committed ones. Mac, 12 VPR runs at a time.

Checks with the pass off (before trusting anything):
- New VPR, option off: conv_layer s1 at 1.62x routed gives 21.7525 ns, wirelength 227507 at width
  142, the committed row. Packing with the criticality file written gives a byte-identical .net;
  packing, then placing and routing separately, gives the same 21.7525 ns and 227507.
- compare_2d_3d.py with the new flags, pass off, width from the CSV: conv_layer and spmv s1 at 1.0x
  routed reproduce their headline rows exactly (19.8798 ns / 225817; 15.7643 ns / 122137).
- layerpass_table.py pointed at ../crossings reproduces crossings_table.txt (0.964 / 0.957 at 1.62x,
  0.982 / 0.970 with pins).
- The pass checks every rewrite: each FLE reads the same nets and FLEs at the same inputs as before,
  through its own layer's crossbar and pins.

== Step 1: are the crossings on the top paths avoidable? (measure_avoidable.py; conv_layer and
eltwise_layer seed 1 at 1.62x, rerun with every output kept; all 8 runs equal their committed rows.)

barely exist and any fix swaps FLEs. Per-crossing check: every crossing on the top 10 paths has a
free slot or a swap partner on the other layer that is on none of those paths. Joint check ("unique ... -> ..."):
per CLB, all its top-path FLEs re-placed at once (10 per layer), other CLBs fixed: most crossings
on these paths go away, path 1's crossing delay 2493 -> 0 ps (conv, routed). An upper bound: it
ignores every connection off these paths, and placement and routing change. Enough to build the pass.

== Reading
- The worst-case edge moved. At 1.62x + pins, split over stacked goes from 0.982 to 0.952 with ideal
  clocks (1.8% -> 4.8% faster, 6/7 designs, every seed's geomean below 1: 0.923-0.966) and from
  0.970 to 0.955 routed (3.0% -> 4.5%). Without conv_layer: 1.002 -> 0.972 and 0.990 -> 0.977, so
  the tie becomes a 2.8% / 2.3% edge.
- Routed clocks are the weak spot: at 1.62x + pins only 3/7 designs beat stacked with the pass
  (5/7 without). bwave_like and eltwise_layer improve but stay slower (1.049 -> 1.015, 1.048 ->
  1.029), attention_layer and robot_rl turn slower (0.982 -> 1.017, 0.985 -> 1.010). And
  compare_configs.py's rule (drop any design with a failed run: conv_layer and robot_rl here) gives
  0.970 with or without the pass. (Superseded by A below: with the failed runs rerun at 200 router
  iterations nothing is dropped, 0.954, and 4/7 designs beat stacked.)
- Over the defended range (1.0-1.62x) with the pass: 0.924-0.938 ideal, 0.933-0.940 routed, i.e.
  6.2-7.6% and 6.0-6.7% (without conv_layer 0.940-0.956, 0.957-0.963). The pass is worth more as
  crossings get slower: on / off 0.988 at 1.0x, 0.974 at 1.62x, 0.970 at 1.62x + pins (ideal).
- It is the layers, not the reshuffle: the control (same rewrite, no FLE changes layer) gives 1.006
  ideal / 1.000 routed, and the pass beats the control by 0.968 / 0.973. Wirelength is unchanged
  (0.996-1.002), tracks are the same, and the pass nearly halves the crossings on the critical path
  at 1.62x routed (3.29 -> 1.78; stacked 0.57) and cuts them by about a third with ideal clocks
  (3.99 -> 2.73; stacked 0.59).
- attention_layer gets slightly slower with the pass in 5 of 6 sets (on / off 1.010-1.035; 0.998 at
  1.62x + pins ideal; 1.017 / 1.032 against the control at 1.62x), though its packer-critical
  crossings drop (566 -> 330 between CLBs).
  Likely cause, not tested: its critical paths run through RAM/DSP, whose connections the pass
  ignores, and the packer's timing model (one fixed delay per net) does not rank them well.
- What is still not done: the pass does not change which LUTs share a CLB (packing itself is
  still layer-blind), ignores hard-block and IO connections, and uses the packer's coarse
  criticality. The split's critical path still crosses 3-5 times as often as stacked's (1.78 vs
  0.57 routed, 2.73 vs 0.59 ideal, at 1.62x).
